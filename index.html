The next step in style preferences

Recommendation apps usually guess your taste from what you browse. That tells them what you'd click on, not what you'd wear. Dapper learns from what you own and what you do with it: the outfits you save, the ones you actually wear, and the ones you reject.

And it learns carefully. Turning down an outfit doesn't mean you hate every piece in it. Usually it's the combination that's off. So Dapper remembers combinations separately from individual garments, keeps what you told it apart from what it inferred, and never turns one passing chat comment into a permanent rule. Over time your style profile becomes a real picture of you, built from your own choices rather than an ad funnel.

A walk through the app

Every screenshot below is from the real Android build, captured on an emulator. Tap any screen to look closer. The people and clothes are synthetic images we generated for testing, not real people.

01

A friendly start

Sign up, and a short tour explains the four ideas that matter: scan instead of typing, confirm what the app guessed, zoom in on anything, and keep control of your data. You can replay it from your profile whenever you like.

02

Home that knows what to do next

A setup checklist shows exactly what's left to get started. Below it, Today's pick is an outfit already put together from your clothes, tuned to the weather. One tap says "I'm wearing this", and Dapper remembers.

03

Getting clothes in, fast

Lay a few things on the bed and take one photo, or pick up to twenty from your gallery. Each item is found and cut out on the phone. When the app is confident it says so ("looks like leggings, 80% sure"). When it isn't, it asks instead of pretending, and offers a one-tap "maybe jeans?" hint.

04

A wardrobe you can actually browse

A clean grid with filters for things that need review, favourites, laundry and each category. Long-press to select several and confirm, wash or remove them together (with Undo). Open any piece to edit it, or pinch-zoom right into the fabric.

05

Outfits, explained

Pick an occasion and swipe through options. Every outfit comes with a score and the reasons behind it, in plain words. Or just tell the stylist what you need, then refine: it keeps what you asked to keep and changes the rest.

06

Plan the week, know your wardrobe

Choose an occasion for each day and Dapper plans the week without repeating pieces. Insights show what you wear, what you've forgotten, and where the gaps are, so the next thing you buy actually earns its place.

07

See it on you

Add one full-body photo and put on anything from your wardrobe or the sample catalog. The quick preview is instant and runs entirely on the phone. "Enhance realism" then adds believable folds and lighting, without touching your face or hair. Compare before and after, zoom in, or share the look.

08

Your profile, your rules

Set colours you love and colours you'll never wear. Every optional permission has its own switch, all off until you choose otherwise. Delete everything in one tap, and it's really gone.

How it works

Three engines, each doing one job well, instead of one giant AI that's confidently wrong.

1 · From photo to wardrobe

  1. Photoone shot, many items
  2. FindML Kit object detection crops each item
  3. Nameimage labels mapped to clothing types
  4. Colourreal colour science (CIELAB), not guesswork
  5. You confirm"Needs review" until you say so

All of this happens on your phone. Duplicates are caught when you upload ("you already have this one"), and anything the app isn't sure about is marked unknown rather than invented.

2 · The outfit engine

Hard rules first

Unavailable, excluded or doubled-up items are never suggested. A dress doesn't need trousers. An athletic outfit needs proper shoes.

Then a balanced score
Colour 20%
Formality 20%
Silhouette 15%
Occasion 15%
Pattern 10%
Season 10%
Your taste 10%
Then variety

The top five aren't five versions of the same thing. Each pick comes with plain-language reasons taken straight from the scores.

3 · Try-on, in layers

Tier A · on the phone · ~0.3 s

Fit

Google MediaPipe finds 33 body landmarks and which pixels are hair, skin, face and clothing. Garments are shaped to your body (sleeves along your real arms, the torso tapered from shoulder to hip) and layered by type.

Tier B · optional · a few seconds

Realism

Each garment is cut into a square tile, regenerated by a Stable Diffusion model only inside the garment, and blended back. Your face and hair are masked out, so they're always your own pixels.

Tier C · planned

True try-on model

Reserved for a dedicated try-on model with a licence that allows shipping. Today's best ones are research-only.

Measured on our test photos: 97 to 100% of face and hair pixels are unchanged after the fit, and the realism pass changes none of them.

Dapper and Glance AI

Glance AI is the best-known app in this space, and it's genuinely impressive. It turns one selfie into a daily stream of AI-generated photos of you in new outfits, tuned to things like the weather, and lets you shop similar looks from hundreds of brands, even from a Samsung lock screen. It's polished, fast and huge: Glance says its beta produced over 40 million looks for 1.5 million people.

Why we think this is the next step

Generating a flattering picture of you is the easy half of the problem, and it mostly ends in a shopping cart. The harder, more useful half is understanding your style well enough to dress you from the closet you already have. That's a richer signal: it's what you chose to own, what you keep reaching for, and what you quietly avoid. Dapper treats that signal with care. It explains itself, admits when it's unsure, and keeps the result yours.

We're not pretending to be further along than we are. Glance AI is a finished product used by millions; Dapper is an early build. But the direction is different on purpose: from "show me something new" to "know me, and help me wear what I have, better."

The fine print, in plain words

  • Try-on is a visual aid. It doesn't simulate fit, size or fabric.
  • Clothing recognition uses Google ML Kit's general labels, so it's sometimes unsure. That's why it asks you to confirm.
  • The on-phone realism engine is based on Local Dream, which is licensed for non-commercial use. Dapper is currently a personal project, and it isn't bundled with the app.
  • Every person and garment shown here is a synthetic image generated for testing. No real people's photos were used.
